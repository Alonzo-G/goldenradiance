## How to verify anything on this page

Four things separate a real supplier document from a bought one, and all four
apply to us. If a claim on this site cannot be checked against these, it should
not be published — and if it is, write to us and we will either produce the
evidence or remove the claim.

1. **Standard and method.** Which standard, and which wear simulation where the
   article is coated.
2. **Laboratory.** Cross-check the report number with the issuing laboratory
   directly.
3. **Batch.** Plating baths drift. A report from six lots ago says nothing about
   the lot you are receiving.
4. **Date.** A report without a date is not evidence of anything current.

## Certificates and standards

This site documents standards and testing methods, and explains what a given
report does and does not prove. We do not publish site-wide certificate badges
on product pages, and the reason is written down in
[Nickel release and EN 1811](/compliance/nickel-release-en-1811/): a limit printed
next to a style without a report asks you to assume a result.

What we can do, per order:

- Material test reports issued against the batch you order.
- Nickel release testing to EN 1811, with EN 12472 wear simulation for coated
  articles.
- The stricter 0.2 µg/cm²/week limit for pierced articles such as ear posts.
- Documentation issued for your destination market, stated in your quotation.

Each is issued against a real batch and travels with the quotation. State your
destination market in the RFQ notes and the matching documentation is what you
get back.

## What we do not publish, and why

Three figures are deliberately absent from this site: minimum order quantity,
material composition per style, and production lead time. Not because the
information is unavailable, but because they are confirmed against your actual
specification and order, and publishing a single number that does not apply to
every style on the site would be worse than saying so.

That is a deliberate trade, not an omission. You get the figure in writing with
your quotation, against the SKU you actually order.

## Third-party verification

Verification is not limited to documents. You are welcome to use your own
inspection party before shipment, or to visit before placing a first order.
Third-party inspection is accepted on production orders, and the scope is agreed
in writing with the quotation — including whether inspection is your cost or
ours.

## What we need from you to quote accurately

The more specific you are, the faster the quotation comes back. Per style or
program, the useful inputs are:

- The styles or a style category, and target quantity per style.
- Material and finish direction, if you have one.
- Packaging requirement — bulk poly bag, backing card, or branded packaging.
- Destination market and destination port.
- Whether you need samples first, and any deadline.
- For OEM or ODM work: artwork, logo placement, or a reference sample.

An RFQ that says "1000 pieces, mixed styles, ship to Rotterdam" gets a slower
and vaguer answer than one that says what you need. We would rather ask once and
reply once.
