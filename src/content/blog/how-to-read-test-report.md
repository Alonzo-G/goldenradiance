A test report is not a certificate of virtue. It is a measurement, made by a
laboratory, on a sample, at a point in time. Four attributes make it usable as
evidence — and any one missing makes it decoration.

## The four attributes

1. **The standard** — which method was applied. For coated jewelry, nickel
   release must be tested after EN 12472 wear simulation; a report citing only
   EN 1811 does not cover the article as sold.
2. **The laboratory** — who made the measurement. Cross-check the report
   number with the issuing laboratory directly; this single step filters most
   fabricated paperwork.
3. **The report number** — the traceable identifier that ties the document to
   a specific measurement.
4. **The date** — when the measurement was made. Plating baths drift; a report
   from six lots ago says nothing about the lot you are buying.

## Per-batch or not at all

This is why our compliance position is per-batch testing: the report for the
matching alloy, plating and batch travels with your quotation. A site-wide
"compliant" badge would be a claim about products that have not been made yet —
which is precisely the kind of claim a buyer should refuse to accept.
