316L and 304 are both austenitic stainless steels, and both are sold as
"stainless steel jewelry". The practical difference is molybdenum: 316L adds it
for chloride resistance.

## Why chloride resistance matters

Sweat, pool water and sea water are all chloride environments. Chlorides attack
the passive layer on stainless steel; molybdenum makes that layer hold. This is
why 316L — not 304 — is the grade used for pieces worn against skin on our
stainless and titanium steel line.

## What buyers should check

Grade claims are only as good as the incoming inspection behind them. On every
spec sheet for this line, the base material grade is a stated field, verified at
incoming inspection — not a marketing word on a category page.

## The same logic applies to plating claims

A grade number without verification is a story. Ask how the grade is checked,
on which lots, and what documentation travels with the shipment. If the answer
is "trust us", you have learned something — just not about the steel.
